## Intended behavior

A publication keeps its configuration and its output records under its own
`publication/<name>/` folder, created on first use, and never writes under
`plans/`. Its `config.yaml` declares a bounded `reads` allow-list, so the
publication reads only the artifact types it names and never scans. Each published
plan gets one authoritative record under `published/`, keyed by the stable plan id
and made idempotent by a per-task `synced_digest`.

## Concrete change and affected surfaces

Author `wrapper/contracts/schemas/publication-config.yaml` describing the
`config.yaml` shape: `schema_version`, `publication`, `kind`, `provider`,
`direction: export`, `trigger: manual`, optional free-text `instructions`, the
bounded `reads` list, credential-free `target_ref`, and the kind-specific
`mapping` (plan->work-item, task->child-item, `acceptance_as`, `status` map).
Author `wrapper/contracts/schemas/publication-record.yaml` for the plan kind's
record: `plan`, `provider`, `plan_item`, and `task_items` each with `task`, `id`,
`url`, and `synced_digest`. Both schemas are credential-free and keep records under
the publication folder, never under `plans/`.

## Inputs and outputs

Inputs: the isolation invariants from EXT-001 and the config/record shapes in the
design. Outputs: two new schema files owning `publication_config` and
`publication_record`. No credentials appear in either shape; `target_ref` holds
only a logical destination.

## Risks and open questions

- `direction` and `trigger` exist as fields so the contract can refuse anything
  other than `export`/`manual`; the schema must make non-conforming values
  rejectable rather than silently accepted.
