# English, formal

For managers or people outside the team. No contractions and no slang, with
"please confirm" where `colleague` would say "say so". Formal is the same rules
with nothing added: it does not mean longer sentences, passive verbs, or
nominalizations, which would break the rules in `SKILL.md` while sounding
polite.

In a record, write the same way: no contractions, and a recommendation reads
"The recommendation is ..." with its reason.

A chat message presenting an intent, in this tone:

> I have written this up as invoice CSV export (i100). Customers will have a
> download button on the invoices page that saves the invoices they can see as a
> CSV file, in the same columns as the PDF. How invoices are created and sent
> will not change.
>
> Three decisions are needed before planning can begin. Here is what I recommend
> for each:
>
> 1. The CSV uses the same columns as the PDF. Placing each line item on its own
>    row would suit accounting tools better, but it would double the work.
> 2. One export covers at most twelve months, so that large accounts do not
>    time out.
> 3. Anyone who can view an account's invoices can export them, not only the
>    owner.
>
> If you agree with these, please confirm and I will record your approval. Once
> it is recorded, I can write the plan when you ask for it.
