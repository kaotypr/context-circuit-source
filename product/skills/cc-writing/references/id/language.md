# Bahasa Indonesia

Compose in Bahasa; never write it in English first and translate. Translated
Bahasa is grammatical and still reads wrong, and it comes from the English
sentence shape more than from the words: English phrases carried over one by
one, English clause order, and English nouns where Bahasa would use a verb. The
reader to write for is an engineer on a Jakarta team, in Slack or in a doc.

## Say what happens, not the English phrase

A phrase lifted from English reads oddly even when every word is right. Write
what actually happens instead:

- "Halaman docs ikut dapat button" is "the docs pages pick up the buttons".
  Write "button di halaman docs disamakan dengan home page".
- "Install langsung di atas" is "install right up top". Write "command
  install-nya ditampilkan paling atas, jadi pengunjung bisa langsung mulai".
- "Kayak X" or "seperti X" reads as making a copy of X. When the meaning is
  taking its style and feel, write "gayanya mirip X" or "senada dengan X".

## Sentence shape

Name the topic first, then say what about it: "soal X: ...", "Spacing-nya beda
di tiap card". Mark something already known with -nya (button-nya, test-nya,
PR-nya). Join short clauses with jadi, karena, tapi, kalau, and terus rather
than building one long sentence.

Leave out the forms that mark text as translated: merupakan; sebuah used like
"a"; di mana or yang mana as "where" or "which"; hal ini or hal tersebut opening
a sentence; melakukan or dilakukan before a noun (write dicek, not dilakukan
pengecekan); memiliki where punya or ada fits; apabila; and openers like oleh
karena itu, dengan demikian, selain itu, lebih lanjut, namun demikian, and
penting untuk dicatat bahwa.

## Words

Use the word the team uses for a thing, whether that is Indonesian (jabatan,
cabang, resi, pengguna) or English (deploy, branch, field, card, spacing), and
translate neither way. English verbs take Indonesian affixes as they do in
speech: di-load, dideploy, di-review, di-approve, ter-copy. Pick one spelling
for each and keep it through the text.

kalau is the everyday "if"; jika belongs to `formal` only.

## Records and chat

A record addresses nobody, calls the team kita, and states a recommendation
without a pronoun: "Rekomendasinya ...". A record follows these rules at the
`colleague` level whatever the tone, unless the tone file says otherwise.

In chat, address the member by their member ID, which is the nickname they
chose. Never add Mas, Mba, Pak, Bu, or Kak, and never use Anda. Use at most one
particle in a message (ya, deh, sih, nih). Ask with a spoken question, such as
"... aman?", "... kan?", or "boleh minta jawabannya?", not a formula like
"Mohon arahannya".
